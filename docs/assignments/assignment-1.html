a) Seminar Summary: Data Science and Industrial Engineering Insights1

Data analytics is categorized into four main stages, each increasing in complexity and business value. It begins with Descriptive Analytics, which focuses on interpreting historical data. This is followed by Diagnostic Analytics to understand the root causes of events. Then, Predictive Analytics uses this data to forecast future trends. Finally, Prescriptive Analytics provides actionable suggestions and optimization to guide decision-making.

For a data professional to truly understand their impact, they must connect these technical steps to the broader business context. The core of any project lies in defining the problem and its Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) in a quantifiable manner for the end-user.

The seminar highlights that the success of a model is not just measured by its mathematical accuracy, but by its delivery and adoption by the user. Several criteria are essential for this bridge:

  • Explainability & Transparency: Solutions that are easy to explain are more useful in a business environment.

  • Quantifiable Results: It is crucial to mathematically demonstrate the power of a solution. This can be achieved by creating charts that compare current KPIs against improvements or by running different scenario analysis to show the logic behind the results.

  • User Interface (UI) & Feedback: A simple, clean UI is vital. Using a “standard language” (standardized KPIs) and gathering early feedback ensures the model meets the user’s actual needs.
